Read the relation and its rendering boundaries

EI specification → AI transcription. The relation and terms are supplied by Joe; this page is an explicit, inspectable transcription. “Binary” here means a relation between addressed things. It does not replace those things with 0s and 1s.

child frame = parent frame × face placement × face orientation × local-axis turn

  • The child’s bottom (local −Y) faces its parent. Its top (local +Y) points away. The child’s local turn is applied after its face orientation, within the frame carried by its parent.
  • All six face addresses recur, including the inward face. Distinct addresses can occupy the same place or overlap ancestors. They are retained, never merged or removed because of overlap.
  • “Fracture = grain” is shown as a finite point-by-point subdivision of the selected cube. Each point has a stable rational address. This inspection uses nested dyadic point samples: increasing refinement inserts points and retains every prior point. This is a finite display convention, not a prescribed canonical spacing law or a claim about volumetric cells. The same point recipe is available at every cube; only the selected cube’s grain is drawn.
  • The pattern exists before any input. Input fills an address without changing its geometry or identity. Entries stay in this page’s memory until it is closed or reloaded; they are not uploaded or saved to the ledger.
  • Depth and grain resolution bound this rendering. They are not an endpoint claimed for the relation. Size ratio, face-contact spacing, initial orientation, speeds and one-frame step are explicit illustration choices. The default ratio is 1: equal-sized cubes. Scale changes are inspection aids.
  • This is a geometric relation visualization. It makes no physical claim and does not supply or replace the canonical viewer’s unresolved metric law.
