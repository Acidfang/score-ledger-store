Read the relation and its rendering boundaries

EI specification and implementation boundary. Joe’s EI specification assigns AI only source-occurrence transcription. This page is an illustrative implementation of the stated geometric relations; it does not establish a complete EI implementation. “Binary” here means a relation between addressed things. It does not replace those things with 0s and 1s.

Segmented-sphere requirement and display boundary. The relation has N sphere segments and N children: one whole sphere, two hemispheres, and boundaries meeting at top and bottom. This page now repeats any selected count from 1 to 118. Child centres lie at equal radius from their parent. General-N directions use a deterministic Fibonacci spherical distribution as the explicit numerical approximation for the stated even-spreading requirement, not an exact derivation of segment boundaries. The optional sphere and radial lines are guides; full segment boundary surfaces are not implemented.

Direction conventions. One child uses local +Y; two use ±Y. Four retain tetrahedral directions; six retain ±X, ±Y, ±Z. Other counts use the spherical approximation. These choices do not assert that every pair of children is equally spaced. For six, adjacent distances are √2 times the radius and opposites are twice it.

Observer and path frames. The default observer turns with the root parent. At zero relative view turn, the parent has zero relative turn while its absolute model rotation continues. World view uses an external frame. Parent-relative paths remove the root rotation recorded at each sample’s own time; world paths use the original world positions. Switching view does not change the model or rewrite history. Samples without a recorded root phase remain world-only. Background reference points stay fixed in world coordinates and move on screen only through the camera. The observer and reference points are excluded from all centre counts.

Centres first. Trails connect actual sampled world positions from the running transform, in time order. They are not parent–child connectors or invented curves. Root remains at its fixed centre. Changing count, depth or display scale starts a new path segment and retains earlier geometry separately within a finite history budget. No path joins across the geometry change. Time, rotation phase, camera view, filled values and address-keyed drafts survive these changes. Newly exposed centres may lie outside the view; use Fit to reframe. Reset motion clears history. Rate changes preserve history. Camera moves only change the view.

Inherited colour. Each child adds its ordered segment/child-index colour contribution to the contributions retained from all its ancestors. The same child-index colour order repeats at every generation; the visible colour is their equal-weight RGB average. The broad RGB hue palette identifies segment origin and ancestry; it has no wavelength calibration. Palette and blending are display conventions.

The whole pattern inside its primary centre. A second instance repeats all current centre paths at a smaller scale inside root. Select “Finer pattern inside root”, then “Focus centre” to inspect it. Both instances share the same relation and motion parameters; addresses retain their instance prefix. One inner instance is rendered, not an infinite limit. Its scale fits the finite pattern inside root and is an explicit display convention.

Anchored inspection. Wheel zoom holds the world point under the cursor. When the spherical guide is visible, the nearest ray-intersected spherical guide supplies that point. Otherwise a nearby centre’s depth, or the selected centre’s viewing plane, supplies its depth. Pinch holds that point under the moving midpoint. Ongoing model motion remains independent.

child frame = parent frame × radial placement × direction orientation × local-axis turn

  • Every centre is a ZERO POINT. The line from a child’s ZERO POINT to its immediate parent’s ZERO POINT defines its local rotation axis, repeated at each generation; these axes need not be globally collinear. The child’s bottom (local −Y) faces its parent. Its top (local +Y) points away. The child’s local turn is applied after its direction orientation, within the frame carried by its parent. A stable right-handed display basis aligns local +Y with each radial direction; its roll is an illustration convention.
  • The chosen count and indexed direction pattern repeat at every centre. Every child is retained in every displayed generation. Distinct addresses can occupy the same place or overlap ancestors. They are retained, never merged or removed because of overlap.
  • The pattern exists before any input. Input fills an address without changing its geometry or identity. Entries stay in this page’s memory until it is closed or reloaded; they are not uploaded or saved to the ledger.
  • Depth and trajectory history bound this rendering. They are not an endpoint claimed for the relation. Size ratio, radial spacing, initial orientation, speeds and one-frame step are explicit illustration choices. The default ratio is 1: equal-sized local fields. Scale changes are inspection aids.
  • This is a geometric relation visualization. It makes no physical claim and does not supply or replace the canonical viewer’s unresolved metric law.
